Research Profile

Biological traits, quantitative imaging, and colour measurement

My academic training spans pomology, ornamental horticulture, and turfgrass science at China Agricultural University, the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, and Hainan University.

My research has focused on fig floral development and peel coloration, as well as tree-peony flowering under controlled conditions. I have used microscopy, ImageJ/Fiji, histology, and colour spectrophotometry to quantify biological traits across cellular and organ levels. I am interested in extending this experience toward image-based assessment and data-informed analysis of horticultural and postharvest traits.

Selected Research Contributions

Key outcomes at organ, tissue, cellular, and computational scales

Instrument-Based Colour Phenotyping & CIELAB

Investigated fig peel-colour development in a first-author study (Hortic. Plant J., 2026), using a colour spectrophotometer and CIELAB parameters (L*, a*, and b*) to quantitatively track colour changes and relate them to DNA-methylation and gene-expression patterns.

Multi-Scale Morphometrics & Biological Imaging

Quantified sex-specific floral development in fig at organ, tissue, and cellular scales using developmental photography, stereomicroscopy, histology, and scale-calibrated image morphometry in ImageJ/Fiji, establishing high-resolution cytological datasets.

Controlled Tree-Peony Forcing & Flower Phenotyping

Conducted controlled forcing experiments and systematically quantified flowering performance, including flowering rate, floral deformity, and CIELAB petal-colour traits, contributing to a first-author publication on DNA demethylation-induced flowering (Int. J. Mol. Sci., 2022).

AI-Assisted Scripting & Candidate Gene Mapping

Integrated outputs from differential-expression analysis, WGCNA, phenotypic associations, and DAP-seq to prioritize FcAGM1 as a candidate regulator; applied AI-assisted scripting to perform downstream genomic coordinate analysis and visualization of candidate genes.
